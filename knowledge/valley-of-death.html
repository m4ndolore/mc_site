Transition Doctrine

Crossing the Valley of Death

The prototype worked. The end user wanted it. The company still died waiting for money that was never in the budget.

— The default outcome, unless you engineer against it

Scroll
01
Structure

The valley is engineered

Two-year budget latency built it

The gap between a demonstrated prototype and a production contract is a funding-mechanics problem before it is a product problem. Production dollars are programmed through PPBE roughly two years before they are spent — the budget year your prototype matures, the money that could buy it was allocated while you were writing your Phase I. Companies that treat transition as a sales problem discover the mechanics too late.

02
Evidence

Evidence outranks advocacy

Build the operator record

A program office moves on documented evidence: exercise outcomes, operator assessments, sustainment data from real use. Enthusiastic end users without documentation move nothing. Every demo, exercise slot, and deployment should produce artifacts a PM can put into a budget justification — that record is what converts operator interest into a budget line.

03
Destination

Pick your program of record early

Transition needs a home

Production money lives in programs of record. Your capability either lands inside one, creates a new start — rare and slow — or stays a demo. Identify the POR whose gaps you fill, learn its budget lines and its PM's transition risks, and shape your roadmap to their milestones. A prototype with a named destination reads as a transition plan. A prototype without one reads as a science project.

04
Authority

Phase III is broader than founders think

Use the sole-source lineage

Any work that derives from SBIR/STTR lineage qualifies for Phase III — sole-source, any agency, any appropriation, no recompete. Program offices use it when the paperwork trail is clean. Keep your data rights and lineage documented from day one. That authority is the widest legal bridge across the valley, and most founders never invoke it.

05
Endurance

Fund the crossing itself

Bridge revenue is oxygen

Even a clean transition takes 18 to 36 months. Companies cross on bridge revenue: follow-on SBIRs, OTA prototype extensions, prime subcontracts, and commercial dual-use sales that keep the team alive while the budget cycle catches up. Plan the bridge before you need it — raising capital against an open valley reads as distress, and it prices like it.
